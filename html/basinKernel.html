shLowLevel.basinKernel

Band-limited basin kernels from polygons, masks, or functions — with great-circle buffering and spectral tapering.

Syntax

[b, info] = shLowLevel.basinKernel(idx, region)
[b, info] = shLowLevel.basinKernel(idx, region, BufferKm=300, TaperKm=350)

Description

Builds the spherical-harmonic coefficient vector of the (band-limited) indicator function of a region by exact Gauss–Legendre quadrature, b = Y' * (w .* mask), in the ordering of idx = shLowLevel.shIndex(...). The result feeds ts.basinAverage(b) and shLowLevel.basinDeconvolve.

Inputs

regionhandle | Kx2 double | Ngrid x 1vectorized f(latDeg,lonDeg) in [0,1]; polygon vertices [lat lon]; or a mask on the quadrature grid
BufferKm(1,1) double, 0>0 grows / <0 shrinks the region by exact great-circle distance before quadrature
TaperKm(1,1) double, 0spectral Jekeli-Gaussian taper of the kernel (soft edges, less ringing/leakage)

Best practice: match TaperKm to the smoothing radius of your coefficient filter; use BufferKm to trade leakage-in against leakage-out. Theory and worked examples: workflow guide, “Basin averages”.

Tailored sensitivity kernels v3.7.0

basinKernel returns the band-limited exact indicator: perfect recovery from perfect data. Real data are noisy, and the indicator has energy at every degree, so it amplifies the high-degree error without limit. The usual remedy is to smooth — which trades noise against leakage by picking a radius and hoping.

shLowLevel.sensitivityKernel makes the trade explicit, minimising leakage plus Alpha times propagated noise, subject to unit response over the basin. Closed form via one Lagrange multiplier — no iteration, no stopping criterion. This is the construction behind the ESA CCI and GravIS gridded ice products.

for a = logspace(-2, 4, 20)                    % sweep, then choose
    [k, s(end+1)] = shLowLevel.sensitivityKernel(idx, basin, ...
        Alpha = a, Noise = sigmaVec);
end
loglog([s.leakage], [s.noise])                 % the L-curve; take the corner
The unit-response constraint is what makes it an average. Without it the cheapest way to cut noise is to shrink the kernel towards zero, and the "optimal" kernel measures almost nothing — during development info.gain fell to 0.28 at Alpha = 0.1. Check info.gain: the constraint holds it at 1.

How much better than a Gaussian? At matched noise and matched gain, between 2% and 16% less leakage across plausible far-field weightings — largest, counter-intuitively, for a high-degree weighting where a Gaussian's fixed shape is furthest from optimal. Measure it for your own case rather than quoting a headline. A real error covariance in Noise is what makes the kernel tailored rather than merely smoothed.
