Leakage correction — putting back what the filter removed

Every GRACE filter removes real signal and smears the rest across basin boundaries. A 6° disc under a 500 km Gaussian keeps about half its peak, and what it loses reappears outside its own boundary as leakage. Two corrections are available, and they differ in what you have to believe.

Iterative forward modelling

Find the mass field that, pushed through the same chain the data saw, reproduces the observation:

mk+1 = mk + Gain · (observed − F(mk)),   F = analysis → filter → synthesis
ewh = g.synthesis(-89:89, 0:359, quantity = "ewh", kn = kn);
[m, info] = shLowLevel.leakageCorrect(ewh, -89:89, 0:359, ...
    Filter = "gauss300", Mask = basinMask, Gain = 2);

Filter must be the filter actually applied to the data ("none", "gauss<km>", "DDKn", or a W struct from readDDK / designFilter). Getting it wrong is the one way to make the result meaningless while it still looks plausible.

The mask does the heavy lifting

Confining the solution to pixels where mass can exist is the standard variant and much better conditioned: the iteration must then explain the observation with mass inside the region, so leakage outside is removed rather than redistributed. On a synthetic disc with an exact mask the correction recovers 0.9998 of the truth and leaves exactly zero outside.

The mask must cover every region that can hold mass, not only the one you are measuring. A mask drawn around the target alone tells the iteration that mass exists nowhere else, so signal from neighbouring sources is forced into the target and the answer comes out too large. Include the neighbours, then integrate the corrected field over the target.

How much this matters

Against the published GravIS Greenland basin-average series (COST-G RL02, −231.1 Gt/yr over the matching span), with all four GravIS corrections applied to the same Level-2 input:

MethodGt/yrvs published
naive grid integral, no correction−170.3−26%
forward modelling, Greenland-only mask−259.8+12%
forward modelling, union mask (+ Canadian Arctic, Iceland, Svalbard)−242.5+5%

The neighbouring regions absorb −112 Gt/yr in the union solution — mass the Greenland-only mask had nowhere to put and therefore piled onto Greenland. The union mask also converges faster. The residual +5% is consistent with what is still missing here: the S2 161-day alias is not removed, the neighbour outlines are coarse boxes, and GravIS inverts seven drainage basins jointly rather than one region.

Convergence is judged on the change of the solution, not on the residual. With a mask the problem is inconsistent — no field confined to the region can reproduce an observation that has energy outside it — so the residual settles at a nonzero floor while the solution is perfectly converged. Stopping on the residual would report failure on exactly the case the mask exists for.

Gain and iteration count

Small regions under a strong filter converge slowly: the 6° disc needs about 260 iterations at Gain = 1 and about 170 at Gain = 2. Values around 2–3 accelerate safely; large gains diverge, which is detected and raised as shLowLevel:leakageCorrect:diverged rather than returned as a field that looks like a result. The field is accurate well before the step criterion is met — 0.997 of the truth after 80 of those 260 iterations — so a run that stops at MaxIter is usually still usable; check info.step.

Gridded scaling factors

Push a model series through the same chain and regress, per pixel, true on filtered:

k = shLowLevel.gridScaling(modelEWH, -89:89, 0:359, Filter = "gauss300");
corrected = k .* filteredEWH;

The factors are the least-squares gain per pixel, so they restore amplitude over the model's time behaviour rather than pointwise at any single epoch. They are invariant to the model's amplitude and depend only on its pattern — which is the method's standing caveat, not an implementation detail.

Where the model carries no signal, k is NaN. The ratio would be two numerical zeros, and returning a number there would silently multiply your data by noise. info.covered and info.onSignal make a model that does not reach your region visible instead. info.kMedian summarises the pixels the model gives mass to, not all finite pixels — pure-leakage pixels correctly get k = 0 and would otherwise dominate the median.

Validation

Both methods were implemented and checked in Python (tools/dev/validate_leakage.py) before the MATLAB port, against known truth: exact analysis/synthesis round trip (4e-16), zero in / zero out, monotone residual, the divergence bound, amplitude invariance of k to 1e-15, and error reduction on a field whose mixture differs from the model's.
